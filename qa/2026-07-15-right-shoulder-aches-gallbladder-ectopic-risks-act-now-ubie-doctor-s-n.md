Explanation

Right shoulder pain is common. In many cases, it's caused by muscle strain, poor posture, or overuse. But sometimes, right shoulder pain isn't coming from the shoulder at all.

Certain internal conditions — including gallbladder disease and ectopic pregnancy — can cause what's known as referred pain to the right shoulder. This means the pain starts somewhere else in the body but is felt in the shoulder.

Understanding when right shoulder pain could signal something more serious can help you make informed decisions without unnecessary panic.

Why Can Internal Problems Cause Right Shoulder Pain?

The body shares nerve pathways between the abdomen and the shoulder. When certain organs become inflamed or irritated, pain signals travel along the same nerves that supply the shoulder area.

One important nerve involved is the phrenic nerve, which connects the diaphragm (the muscle used for breathing) to the shoulder region. If the diaphragm becomes irritated — often from fluid, inflammation, or internal bleeding — the pain may be felt in the right shoulder.

This is called referred pain.

Right Shoulder Pain and Gallbladder Problems

One of the most common internal causes of right shoulder pain is gallbladder disease, especially gallstones.

How the Gallbladder Causes Shoulder Pain

The gallbladder sits in the upper right abdomen, just under the liver. When gallstones block a duct or the gallbladder becomes inflamed (a condition called cholecystitis), it can irritate nearby nerves and the diaphragm.

Right Shoulder Pain and Ectopic Pregnancy

While less common overall, right shoulder pain in women of reproductive age can sometimes signal an ectopic pregnancy — a potentially life-threatening condition.

What Is an Ectopic Pregnancy?

An ectopic pregnancy occurs when a fertilized egg implants outside the uterus, most often in a fallopian tube. The pregnancy cannot develop normally and may cause internal bleeding if the tube ruptures.

How to Tell If Right Shoulder Pain Is Muscular or Internal

Most right shoulder pain is musculoskeletal. Here's how it often differs:

Likely Muscular Causes

Pain worsens with shoulder movement

Tenderness when pressing on the shoulder

Pain after lifting, exercise, or poor posture

Limited range of motion

Common causes include:

Rotator cuff strain

Tendinitis

Frozen shoulder

Arthritis

Possible Internal (Referred) Causes

Shoulder looks and moves normally

Pain is not triggered by shoulder motion

Accompanied by abdominal symptoms

Associated with nausea, fever, or dizziness

Pain feels deep rather than surface-level

If your shoulder feels structurally normal but you also have abdominal or pelvic symptoms, it's important to consider causes beyond the joint itself.

Other Internal Causes of Right Shoulder Pain

Though gallbladder disease and ectopic pregnancy are two important examples, other internal conditions can also cause right shoulder pain:

Liver inflammation or infection

Pneumonia affecting the lower right lung

Pulmonary embolism

Subphrenic abscess (infection under the diaphragm)

Recent abdominal surgery complications

These are less common but may require urgent evaluation if symptoms are severe.

When to Speak to a Doctor About Right Shoulder Pain

You should speak to a doctor promptly if right shoulder pain is:

Sudden and severe

Accompanied by abdominal pain

Associated with fever

Linked to nausea or vomiting

Occurring during early pregnancy

Paired with dizziness or fainting

Not improving after a few days

Getting progressively worse

Even if symptoms seem mild, persistent or unexplained right shoulder pain deserves medical attention.

What a Doctor May Do

To identify the cause of right shoulder pain, a healthcare provider may:

Ask detailed questions about symptoms

Perform a physical exam

Order blood tests

Perform an ultrasound (especially for gallbladder or pregnancy concerns)

Use CT or MRI imaging if needed

Early diagnosis helps prevent complications.

Key Takeaways

Right shoulder pain is often harmless and related to muscles or joints. However, in certain cases, it can signal internal issues such as:

Gallbladder disease

Ectopic pregnancy

Diaphragm irritation from internal bleeding or infection

Pay attention to additional symptoms. Shoulder pain combined with abdominal discomfort, nausea, fever, or pregnancy-related symptoms should not be ignored.

Scared of Plan B Side Effects? Why Your Body Reacts & Medically Approved Next Steps

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Plan B side effects are usually mild and short-lived, including temporary period changes, nausea, fatigue, headache, breast tenderness, and mild cramping. Plan B does not impact future fertility.

Recommended next steps after taking Plan B:

Track your menstrual cycle

Use condoms until your next period

Take a pregnancy test if your period is more than 1 week late, or about 3 weeks after sex

Seek urgent care for severe lower abdominal pain, very heavy bleeding, or fainting

If you vomit within 2 hours of taking Plan B, ask a pharmacist or doctor about repeating the dose

Because everyone's body responds differently, and symptoms like cramping, bleeding changes, or nausea can overlap with other conditions (including early pregnancy or ectopic pregnancy), it's important to understand what your specific symptoms may mean. Explanation

If you've taken Plan B (also called the "morning-after pill") and you're feeling nervous about Plan B side effects, you're not alone. Many people worry about how their body will react. The good news? Most side effects are temporary, manageable, and not dangerous.

As a medical professional, I want to explain clearly what's happening in your body, what's normal, what's not, and what you should do next.

What Is Plan B and How Does It Work?

Plan B One-Step contains levonorgestrel, a synthetic version of the hormone progesterone. It works mainly by:

Delaying or preventing ovulation (stopping the ovary from releasing an egg)

Thickening cervical mucus to make it harder for sperm to move

Possibly interfering with fertilization

It does not end an existing pregnancy and does not affect an implanted embryo.

Because it delivers a higher dose of hormone than regular birth control pills, your body may temporarily react. That reaction is what causes most Plan B side effects.

3. Cramping and Abdominal Pain

Some people feel cramping after taking Plan B. This is usually mild and temporary.

Why it happens:

Hormonal shifts affect the uterine lining

Ovulation timing may be altered

Mild cramping is normal. Severe, sharp, or one-sided abdominal pain is not normal and needs urgent medical evaluation because it could signal an ectopic pregnancy (rare but serious).

4. Fatigue, Dizziness, and Headaches

Hormones influence your nervous system. A sudden change can cause:

Feeling tired

Brain fog

Lightheadedness

Mild headaches

These symptoms usually resolve within 24–72 hours.

Stay hydrated, rest, and avoid intense physical stress while your body adjusts.

5. Breast Tenderness

Progesterone-like hormones can make breast tissue temporarily sensitive or swollen.

This side effect typically resolves within a few days to a week.

When Plan B Side Effects Are NOT Normal

While most reactions are mild, you should seek medical care immediately if you experience:

Severe lower abdominal pain

Fainting

Does Plan B Affect Future Fertility?

This is a very common fear.

The evidence is clear:Plan B does not affect long-term fertility.

It temporarily delays ovulation. That's it. Once it leaves your system, your fertility returns to baseline.

The Bottom Line on Plan B Side Effects

Here's the honest truth:

Most Plan B side effects are mild and temporary.

Period changes are the most common reaction.

Serious complications are rare.

Your fertility is not harmed.

Your body is adjusting — not malfunctioning.

However, do not ignore severe pain, heavy bleeding, fainting, or signs of pregnancy. Those require immediate medical care.

If anything feels extreme, worsening, or potentially life-threatening, speak to a doctor right away.

Published on: 7/21/2026

Can sleep apnea return after treatment or surgery?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Sleep apnea can return after treatment or surgery, even without weight gain. Recurrence is possible because underlying causes—such as aging, changes in airway anatomy, muscle tone loss, nasal congestion, alcohol use, or shifts in sleep position—can persist or develop over time. Surgical outcomes may also diminish as tissues heal and remodel, and CPAP effectiveness can decline if pressure settings are no longer suited to your current needs. Ongoing monitoring is essential, since untreated recurrent sleep apnea raises risks for heart disease, stroke, and daytime fatigue.

If you're noticing symptoms like loud snoring, gasping during sleep, morning headaches, or persistent tiredness, don't wait to find out what's happening. A free, instant, online symptom check can help you clarify your symptoms, understand possible causes, and guide your next steps—whether that's revisiting your doctor, adjusting treatment, or exploring new options.

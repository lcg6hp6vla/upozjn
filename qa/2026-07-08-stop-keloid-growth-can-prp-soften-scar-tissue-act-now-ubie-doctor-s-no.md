Explanation

Keloids are stubborn, raised scars that form when the body's healing process goes into overdrive. They can be itchy, painful and, for many, a cosmetic concern. Platelet-rich plasma (PRP) has emerged as a promising option in scar management. In this article, we'll explore whether PRP for keloids can soften scar tissue, what the research says, and what you should discuss with your doctor.

What Are Keloids?

Keloids develop when collagen production exceeds what's needed to heal a wound. Key points:

They extend beyond the original injury site.

Common triggers: piercings, acne, surgery, burns, insect bites.

Appear as firm, raised bumps that may be pink, red or darker than surrounding skin.

Can cause itching, tenderness or even pain.

PRP's appeal lies in its natural, autologous origin and its high levels of growth factors such as PDGF, TGF-β and VEGF. The proposed benefits include:

Reducing inflammation around the scar.

Encouraging more organized collagen deposition.

Softening and flattening raised scar tissue.

Improving skin texture and color.

While exact mechanisms remain under study, clinicians believe PRP may shift scar maturation from a "stuck" inflammatory phase toward a more regulated healing process.

What Does the Research Say?

Evidence on PRP for keloids is still emerging. Here's a snapshot of key findings:

• Small pilot studies report scar softening and size reduction after PRP injections combined with other treatments (e.g., steroids or laser).• Some reports suggest PRP may reduce keloid recurrence when used alongside surgical removal.• Controlled trials are limited, and protocols (dose, frequency, combination therapies) vary widely.

Researchers agree more large-scale, randomized studies are needed. However, early data support the idea that PRP could be a useful adjunct in keloid management rather than a stand-alone cure.

What to Expect in a PRP Session

A typical PRP treatment for keloids involves:

Consultation and skin assessment.

Blood draw (about 10–20 mL).

Centrifugation (10–15 minutes).

Injection of PRP into and around the keloid.

Possible application of a topical numbing agent beforehand.

Most sessions take under an hour. You can usually return to normal activities immediately, though you might experience mild soreness.

Cost and Accessibility

PRP therapy costs vary by region and provider, typically ranging from $300 to $1,200 per session. Many clinics offer package deals (e.g., three or five sessions). Since PRP is considered cosmetic in many cases, insurance rarely covers it.

Talking to Your Doctor

Always discuss new treatments with a qualified healthcare provider. When you speak to your doctor, be prepared to cover:

Your keloid history (onset, triggers, previous treatments).

Your goals (e.g., flattening, reducing color, relieving itch).

Any medical conditions or medications (to rule out contraindications).

Budget and willingness to commit to multiple sessions.

If your keloids are causing itching or pain, or if you notice rapid growth, speak to a doctor promptly. While keloids are benign, sudden changes can indicate infection or other issues that require urgent care.

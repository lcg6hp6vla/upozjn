Explanation

Pain under the right armpit can happen for many reasons, and yes, workouts are one common trigger—especially in women, whose shoulder and chest muscles may respond differently to exercise stress than men's. Below is a detailed look at why this pain occurs, how to tell if it's workout-related, other possible causes, and when to get medical advice.

Anatomy of the Right ArmpitThe armpit (axilla) is a busy junction of:

Pectoralis minor and major (chest)

Serratus anterior (side of ribs)

Latissimus dorsi (back)

Brachial plexus (arm nerve bundle)

Blood vessels

Lymph nodes

Because so many structures pass through this small area, pain can arise from muscles, nerves, glands or skin.

How Workouts Can Trigger Pain

Muscle Strain or Micro-tears

Overloading your pectoralis minor, serratus anterior or latissimus dorsi can cause tiny tears.

Common after new exercises (e.g., push-ups, chest flyes, pull-ups).

Feels like a dull ache or sharp twinge under the armpit during or after the movement.

Pectoralis Minor Syndrome

According to Sanders & Annest (2007), the pectoralis minor can compress nerves and vessels under the coracoid process, leading to axillary pain.

Pain radiating from front of shoulder into the armpit

Worse with overhead reaches or heavy lifting

May feel numbness or tingling down the arm

Improper Form and Overuse

Rounding the shoulders, flaring elbows, or arching the back can place extra stress on the armpit muscles.

Repetitive movements without adequate rest lead to inflammation of muscles or tendons.

Muscle Imbalance

Strong chest muscles but weak upper back/shoulder stabilizers shift load to smaller muscles under the armpit.

Correct this with balanced strength training and proper stretching.

When to See a Doctor or Physical Therapist

Pain persists more than 1–2 weeks despite rest and gentle stretching.

You have any red-flag symptoms (see above).

Pain worsens during everyday activities (reaching, lifting a grocery bag).

You'd like a professional assessment of your exercise form and muscle balance.

Preventing Workout-Related Armpit Pain

Warm up thoroughly: arm circles, light band pull-aparts, chest openers.

Focus on proper form: keep shoulders down and back, engage core.

Balance your routine: include pulling exercises (rows) to offset chest work.

Progress gradually: increase weight and reps in small increments.

Stretch and mobilize: gentle pectoral stretches and foam-rolling for the chest and shoulder blades.

Key Takeaways

Yes, workouts can trigger pain under the right armpit in females, most often from muscle strain, pectoralis minor syndrome or improper technique.

Other causes include enlarged lymph nodes, breast tissue issues, skin conditions or referred organ pain—though these are less common in athletes.

Watch for red-flag symptoms (severe pain, lumps, systemic signs) and seek medical care if they appear.

Use balanced training, proper form and gradual progression to prevent recurrence.

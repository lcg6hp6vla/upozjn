What could be the reason for feeling a lump in my throat?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Feeling a lump in your throat—medically called globus sensation—is a common symptom that can result from several underlying causes. The most frequent triggers include muscle tension in the throat, acid reflux (GERD), postnasal drip, anxiety or stress, and inflammation of the throat muscles. In some cases, it may also be linked to thyroid issues or swallowing disorders.

Explanation

Feeling like there is a lump in your throat is called globus sensation. This feeling can happen for different reasons. One reason might be tension or tightness in the muscles of the throat. Sometimes, the muscles in the upper part of the throat can become tight or 'hypertensive', which means they are working too hard or are too tense.

Other medical conditions can also cause this feeling. These conditions may affect how the muscles in your throat work or feel. It is important to know that this sensation is common and often not a sign of something serious.

If you are worried about this feeling or if it doesn't go away, you can check your symptoms to better understand potential causes, and it is a good idea to talk to a doctor. They can help figure out what might be causing it and suggest ways to make it feel better.

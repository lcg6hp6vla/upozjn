# Does Chikungunya Spread in Stagnant Water? Urgent Health Alert | Ubie Doctor's Note

Published on: 7/21/2026

Does Chikungunya virus spread through stagnant water?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Chikungunya virus does not spread directly through stagnant water. However, stagnant water serves as a breeding ground for Aedes mosquitoes (Aedes aegypti and Aedes albopictus), which are the primary carriers of the virus. When these infected mosquitoes bite humans, they transmit Chikungunya, causing symptoms like sudden high fever, severe joint pain, muscle aches, rash, and fatigue.

Because Chikungunya symptoms often mimic those of dengue, Zika, or the flu, it can be difficult to know what's really behind how you're feeling—especially after possible mosquito exposure. Explanation

Chikungunya virus is spread to people through mosquito bites, specifically from mosquitoes that breed in and around stagnant water. While the virus itself does not spread through the water, mosquitoes that carry the virus lay their eggs in standing water, which can be found in places like puddles, flower pots, and containers.

Mosquito Breeding: Stagnant water serves as a breeding ground for mosquitoes. The eggs hatch into larvae, eventually becoming adult mosquitoes capable of spreading the virus.

Virus Transmission: When an infected mosquito bites a person, it can transmit the Chikungunya virus.

Prevention Tips

Remove Standing Water: To reduce the risk, eliminate sources of stagnant water around homes and communities.

Use Mosquito Repellents: Apply repellents to exposed skin and use mosquito nets or screens.

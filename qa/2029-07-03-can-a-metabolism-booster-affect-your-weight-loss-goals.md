Can a Metabolism Booster Affect Your Weight Loss Goals?

We’ve all seen those ads on TV for the latest miracle drug that is supposed to boost your metabolism and help you lose weight quickly, but how true are they? What is metabolism?

The term metabolism is a catch-all term for the body’s various processes to turn foods and drinks into energy. The more quickly and efficiently the body turns food into energy, the better our metabolism is said to be working and the easier it is supposed to be for our bodies to lose weight and keep it off.

However, numerous factors affect our metabolism, from hereditary issues to diet and exercise, the foods and drinks we consume, and our overall health. The factor determining our metabolism functions is our BMR or basal metabolic rate. BMR refers to the number of calories the body needs to sustain life-supporting functions and the rate at which our bodies burn calories while resting. When our BMR is low, we have trouble losing weight. This is why you see ads like “best metabolism booster pills for weight loss”.

Do metabolism booster supplements work?

Before we talk about which metabolism boosters do or do not work, we want to caution that there are no miracle drugs that will magically make you start losing weight overnight. Even effective boosters must be accompanied by diet and exercise to work properly. Simply taking a metabolism booster will show little to no results.

Metabolism boosters contain specific ingredients, and these ingredients are what most of them claim will help you lose weight. Here are a few examples.

Green tea

Green tea is a popular ingredient in many metabolism boosters due to its potential to increase calorie burning and promote weight loss. The catechins and caffeine found in green tea are known to boost metabolism and improve fat oxidation, making it an effective component in weight loss supplements and metabolism-boosting products.

Green tea extract may even be more effective than drinking green tea alone, as it contains a higher concentration of catechins, though the science behind it is still being studied.

Capsaicin

Another chemical found in foods with at least some research showing a potential benefit to weight loss is capsaicin. Capsaicin is a compound found in certain spicy foods like chili peppers that helps the peppers produce heat, and some data suggests these foods are good for weight loss.

Caffeine

Caffeine has been extensively studied as a primary ingredient in coffee, several teas, and other beverages. It has been proven to have some weight loss benefits due to its function as a stimulant.

While ingesting caffeine has been known to lower appetite and improve calorie burn, supplements often contain large amounts of it. Caffeine can be dangerous in large amounts as it can increase heart rate, cause high blood pressure, and have other side effects.

Overall, there is very little evidence that metabolism-boosting supplements have more than a marginal effect on weight loss. Using these supplements for a prolonged period or in large quantities may cause health problems.

Can you boost your metabolism with diet?

Some foods actively boost metabolic rate and can improve weight loss, but these foods work best when paired with a diet and exercise plan. Here are a few examples of metabolism-boosting foods:

High protein foods

We all know that proteins are needed to build muscle, but protein also causes the body to exert more energy to digest these foods. This is why protein-rich foods help increase metabolism

An added benefit of high-protein foods is that they help a person feel fuller more quickly and become less hungry over time.

Mineral-rich foods

Our body needs certain minerals like iron to function properly. Eating mineral-rich foods helps function certain organs, one being the thyroid, the gland that regulates metabolism. A healthy thyroid means a healthy metabolism.

Caffeinated drinks

Earlier in this post, we discussed caffeine’s role in weight loss. Caffeine has been researched and studied many times over the years, and its effects on appetite suppression and boosting metabolism have been proven by science. The best way to safely add caffeine to your diet is not with supplements but by drinking beverages like coffee and certain teas that contain caffeine naturally.

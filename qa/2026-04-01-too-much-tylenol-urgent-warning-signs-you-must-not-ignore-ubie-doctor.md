What are the signs that I’ve taken too much Tylenol?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Tylenol overdose symptoms include nausea, vomiting, stomach pain, loss of appetite, sweating, and fatigue. In severe cases, an overdose can cause serious liver damage, jaundice, confusion, or even liver failure. Symptoms may not appear right away, sometimes taking 12–24 hours to develop, which makes overdose especially dangerous. If you suspect a Tylenol overdose, seek emergency medical care immediately.

Explanation

Taking too much Tylenol, also known as acetaminophen, can be dangerous. Early signs of an overdose often include feelings of nausea, vomiting, and pain in the upper right side of the stomach. You might also feel tired or lose your appetite.

If the overdose is severe, it can cause serious liver damage. Later signs of liver damage may include yellowing of the skin or eyes (called jaundice), confusion, and even a coma. It's very important to get help quickly if you think you or someone else has taken too much Tylenol.

Here are some key points to remember:

Early Symptoms: Nausea, vomiting, stomach pain, tiredness, and loss of appetite.

Severe Symptoms: Yellow skin/eyes, confusion, and possible coma.

Wegovy (semaglutide 2.4 mg) is FDA-approved for weight management, but it also has a powerful effect on blood sugar control. Many Black adults living with type 2 diabetes or prediabetes wonder how quickly Wegovy can lower A1C. How Wegovy Works on A1C Reduction

Wegovy is a glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist. It lowers A1C by:

Increasing insulin secretion when blood sugar is high

Suppressing glucagon release (glucagon raises blood sugar)

Slowing gastric emptying, which blunts post-meal blood sugar spikes

Promoting satiety, leading to weight loss that further improves insulin sensitivity

Although Wegovy is labeled for weight loss, the same mechanism that curbs appetite also improves glycemic control—and clinical trials demonstrate meaningful A1C reductions.

Clinical Evidence on A1C Reduction with Semaglutide

Most A1C data come from semaglutide trials in type 2 diabetes (e.g., SUSTAIN trials) and weight-management studies (e.g., STEP trials). Key findings include:

In people with type 2 diabetes, once-weekly semaglutide (0.5 mg or 1.0 mg—not the full 2.4 mg Wegovy dose) reduced A1C by an average of 1.3%–1.8% over 30–56 weeks.

In non-diabetic or prediabetic adults in the STEP 1 trial, semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly led to A1C reductions of 0.6%–1.0% at 68 weeks, with more pronounced drops in those who started with higher A1C.

Meta-analyses of GLP-1 RAs show average A1C reductions of 1.0%–1.5% at 6 months, with greater effects in patients with higher baseline A1C.

Special Considerations for Black Adults

Black adults are disproportionately affected by type 2 diabetes and may start Wegovy with higher baseline A1C. Evidence suggests:

Similar efficacy across races: Clinical trials of semaglutide show comparable A1C and weight-loss outcomes in Black, Hispanic, and White participants.

Potential for larger absolute drop: Higher starting A1C often yields greater absolute reductions (e.g., a drop of 2% vs. 1%).

Cardiovascular benefits: GLP-1 RAs reduce heart-attack and stroke risk—an important factor since Black adults have higher cardiovascular disease rates.

Access and adherence: Insurance coverage, out-of-pocket cost, and consistent clinic follow-up are critical. Partner with a healthcare team experienced in managing diabetes in Black communities.

Managing Expectations: Side Effects & Lifestyle

While Wegovy is powerful, it's not magic. To maximize A1C reduction:

Maintain a balanced, low-glycemic diet

Engage in regular physical activity (150 minutes/week)

Monitor blood glucose and adjust other diabetes medications under medical guidance

Attend follow-up visits to adjust dose or manage side effects

Common GI side effects include nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and constipation. They often peak during dose escalation and subside over time. Less common but serious risks:

Pancreatitis (persistent severe abdominal pain)

Gallbladder disease (severe upper-right abdominal pain)

Severe hypoglycemia if combined with insulin or sulfonylureas

Possible increased heart rate or kidney injury in dehydration

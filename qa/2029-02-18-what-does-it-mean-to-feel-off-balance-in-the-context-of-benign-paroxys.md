Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV) causes a sudden off-balance or spinning sensation triggered by specific head movements, such as rolling over in bed, tilting your head back, or bending down. This happens when tiny calcium crystals (otoconia) in your inner ear become dislodged and shift into the semicircular canals, disrupting the signals your brain uses to maintain balance. Episodes are typically brief but can feel intense, and may be accompanied by nausea or unsteadiness.

Because dizziness can stem from many different conditions—ranging from inner ear issues to blood pressure changes or neurological concerns—identifying the true cause is essential to getting the right care. Explanation

Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo (BPPV) is a condition that affects balance and causes dizziness. When people with BPPV feel off-balance, it often means they experience a spinning sensation, especially when they move their head in specific directions. This sensation is due to tiny crystals in the inner ear that have moved out of their normal position.

How to Prevent Low Blood Sugar Drops

Wegovy (semaglutide) is a once-weekly prescription medication approved for chronic weight management. It works by mimicking a hormone called GLP-1 that helps regulate appetite and blood sugar. Many people tolerate it well, but some report feeling dizzy, shaky, or weak—symptoms often associated with low blood sugar (hypoglycemia). This guide explains why it happens, how to spot warning signs, and practical steps to prevent blood sugar crashes while on Wegovy.

Keywords: Wegovy and blood sugar crashes

Why Might Wegovy Affect Blood Sugar?

GLP-1 action: Semaglutide slows stomach emptying and increases insulin release after meals. This can lead to a sharper insulin response, especially if you eat a high-carb meal.

Combined medications: If you're also on insulin or a sulfonylurea (e.g., glipizide), the risk of hypoglycemia rises because these drugs directly lower blood sugar.

Reduced food intake: Wegovy often decreases appetite. Skipping meals or eating very little can leave you with too little circulating glucose.

Physical activity: Exercise boosts insulin sensitivity. Without adjusting food intake or medication, workouts can tip you into low blood sugar.

Even though semaglutide alone rarely causes dangerous drops, these factors can combine to produce dizziness, shakiness, sweating, or confusion.

Keep a log to spot patterns of lows.

Adjust Medication with Professional Guidance

Review your insulin or sulfonylurea dosage with your doctor or diabetes educator.

Sometimes a lower dose can maintain blood sugar control without causing lows.

Stay Hydrated

Dehydration can worsen dizziness and impair blood sugar regulation.

Aim for 8–10 cups of water daily, more if you exercise or live in a hot climate.

Moderate Intense Exercise

Discuss with your healthcare provider how to adjust carbs or meds on workout days.

Consider a small pre-exercise snack (e.g., half a banana) if blood sugar is below 100 mg/dL.

Limit Alcohol Intake

Alcohol can block your liver's ability to release glucose, especially if consumed on an empty stomach.

If you drink, pair each alcoholic beverage with food.

What to Do During a Low Blood Sugar Episode

Check your blood sugar (if possible).

3–4 glucose tablets

4 ounces (½ cup) of fruit juice or regular soda

1 tablespoon of honey or sugar

Wait 15 minutes, then recheck your blood sugar.

If still below 70 mg/dL, repeat step 2.

Once normalized, eat a balanced snack or meal to stabilize levels.

Note: If you become unconscious or can't swallow safely, seek emergency help immediately.

Adjusting Your Diet for Stability

Fiber‐rich carbs: Oats, legumes, vegetables slow glucose release.

Lean protein: Chicken, fish, tofu promote satiety and stabilize sugar.

Healthy fats: Olive oil, nuts, seeds add staying power.

When to Talk to Your Doctor

Always involve your healthcare team in managing medication and diet changes. Contact them if you experience:

Frequent lows (more than 2–3 times per week)

Severe symptoms (confusion, sweating, rapid heart rate)

Any episode that interferes with driving or daily activities

Any symptom that feels life threatening

If you're starting or increasing your Wegovy dose and notice dizziness or shakiness, schedule an appointment to review your treatment plan.

Safe Use of Wegovy

Follow the prescribed dosing schedule (increasing dose by 0.25 mg per week initially).

Never adjust Wegovy on your own.

Keep all follow-up labs (e.g., HbA1c if diabetic) and appointments.

Report any troubling side effects promptly.

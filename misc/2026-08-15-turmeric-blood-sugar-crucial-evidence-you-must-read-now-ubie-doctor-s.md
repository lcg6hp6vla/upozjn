What is the evidence supporting turmeric's ability to lower blood sugar levels?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Turmeric may help lower blood sugar levels, thanks to its active compound, curcumin. Research suggests curcumin can improve insulin sensitivity, reduce insulin resistance, and support healthier glucose metabolism—making it a promising natural ally for people managing blood sugar concerns.

However, elevated blood sugar can stem from many underlying causes, and turmeric alone isn't a substitute for proper evaluation. Explanation

Turmeric, a spice commonly used in cooking, contains an active ingredient called curcumin. According to several studies, curcumin may have beneficial effects on blood sugar levels.

Study Insights:

[Doctor's Note](https://benpepsi.com/)

The first study suggests that curcumin or turmeric supplementation can positively affect glycemic control. This means it might help manage blood sugar levels.

Another study reviewed the effects of curcumin on diabetes and found that it could be helpful in managing the condition.

The third study also supports the idea that curcumin may improve how the body controls blood sugar in people with diabetes.

General Findings:

These studies indicate that curcumin might be a useful supplement for people looking to manage their blood sugar levels. However, more research is needed to fully understand its effects and the best ways to use it.

It's important to remember that while turmeric and curcumin show promise, they should not replace prescribed diabetes medications or treatments.

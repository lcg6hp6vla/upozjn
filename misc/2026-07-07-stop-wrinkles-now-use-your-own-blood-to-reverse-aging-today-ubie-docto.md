Explanation

As we age, collagen production slows and the skin's natural ability to repair itself diminishes. Fine lines, wrinkles and sagging skin become increasingly evident. Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP) therapy—often dubbed the "vampire facial"—uses your own blood to jump-start the skin's natural healing processes. Backed by dermatology societies and peer-reviewed studies, PRP for wrinkles is emerging as a safe, minimally invasive way to rejuvenate skin from within.

What Is PRP?

[Own Blood](https://synaptide-lab.snaketongueofficial.com/blog/0443510701.html)

PRP stands for Platelet-Rich Plasma. Here's how it works:

A small sample of your blood is drawn, just like a routine lab test.

The sample is spun in a centrifuge to separate the platelets (rich in growth factors) from red blood cells.

The concentrated platelets—your PRP—are then prepared for application or injection.

Growth factors in PRP signal your body to repair tissue, boost collagen and elastin production, and improve blood flow. Because it uses your own blood, the risk of allergic reaction or rejection is virtually zero.

Potential Risks & Side Effects

While PRP is generally well tolerated, you should be aware of:

Temporary redness, swelling or pinpoint bruising

Mild discomfort at injection or needling sites

Risk of infection (extremely low when performed under sterile conditions)

Not suitable if you have platelet disorders, active infections, uncontrolled diabetes or are pregnant

Always choose a board-certified dermatologist, plastic surgeon or experienced aesthetic provider to minimize risks.

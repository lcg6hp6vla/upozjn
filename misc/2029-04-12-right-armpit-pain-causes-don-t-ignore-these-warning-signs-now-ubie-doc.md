Right armpit pain is uncommon and is most often caused by swollen lymph nodes resulting from infections, though other less frequent causes—such as muscle strain, skin conditions, or nerve irritation—can also be responsible. Identifying the underlying cause is key to finding relief and knowing when to seek care.

When you feel sharp or sore pain in your right armpit, one common reason is swollen lymph nodes. Lymph nodes help your body fight off germs, and sometimes they swell when you have an infection. For example, cat-scratch disease is an infection from a scratch by a cat that can make the lymph nodes in your armpit tender. In other cases, conditions like Castleman's disease, which affects the lymph nodes, might appear as a painless mass but sometimes cause discomfort. In children and adults, lymph node disorders can lead to pain in the armpit if the nodes become inflamed or irritated. Other possible causes might include injuries, strains, or skin infections that affect the sweat glands and tissues in the armpit. Because each situation is different, if you have pain that does not go away after a week or two, or if you notice other symptoms like fever or swelling, you can use a symptom checker to help identify possible causes before talking with your doctor. They will help figure out exactly why the pain is happening and choose the best treatment to help you feel better.

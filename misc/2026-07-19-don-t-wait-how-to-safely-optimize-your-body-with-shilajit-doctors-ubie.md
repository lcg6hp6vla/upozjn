1. What Is Shilajit?

[Body with Shilajit](https://calmvector.dns0663.com/blog/5612569462.html)

Shilajit is a sticky, tar-like substance found primarily in Himalayan and Altai mountain rocks. Over centuries, plant matter and microbial action compressed under high pressure created a mineral-rich resin. Modern testing shows Shilajit contains:

Fulvic and humic acids (powerful antioxidants)

Over 80 trace minerals (iron, magnesium, zinc, selenium)

Dibenzo-α-pyrones (potential mitochondrial boosters)

Researchers believe these compounds work together to support cellular energy production, reduce oxidative stress, and modulate hormone signaling pathways.

2. Hormonal Benefits of Shilajit

Several clinical studies suggest Shilajit may influence hormone levels safely, when used appropriately:

Testosterone Support• A randomized study in healthy men (ages 45–55) found that 250 mg of purified Shilajit twice daily for 90 days led to a significant increase in total testosterone and free testosterone vs. placebo.¹• Possible mechanisms: enhanced mitochondrial function and reduced oxidative damage in Leydig cells (testosterone-producing cells).

Stress Hormone Modulation• Shilajit's fulvic acid content may help regulate cortisol (the "stress hormone"), promoting a calmer stress response without blunting healthy adrenal function.

Thyroid Function• Animal research indicates Shilajit may support balanced thyroid hormone production (T3 and T4), potentially through antioxidant protection of thyroid tissue.

Female Hormonal Health• Preliminary data suggests Shilajit could support menstrual regularity and reduce symptoms of mild hormone imbalance, though more human trials are needed.

¹ Seema Sharma et al., "Clinical study of purified Shilajit on testosterone levels," Journal of Ethnopharmacology, 2016.

3. Prioritizing Hormone Safety

Even natural products can carry risks if misused. To ensure "Shilajit and hormone safety," keep these principles in mind:

Medical History Review• Share your complete health history with your doctor, especially if you have hormone-sensitive conditions (e.g., prostate issues, breast cancer, polycystic ovary syndrome).

Baseline Hormone Testing• Before starting Shilajit, get blood tests for testosterone, estrogen, thyroid hormones, and cortisol. This establishes your baseline and tracks changes.

Drug-Supplement Interactions• If you're on hormone therapy (birth control, TRT, thyroid meds), discuss potential interactions. Some minerals in Shilajit can alter absorption of prescription hormones.

Monitor Side Effects• Common mild reactions: digestive discomfort, headache, or changes in sleep patterns. If you notice any concerning symptoms, pause Shilajit and consult your doctor.

5. Quality, Sourcing, and Dosing Guidelines

To maximize benefits and minimize risks, focus on product quality and proper dosing:

Purity and Testing• Choose brands that provide Certificates of Analysis (COA) for heavy metals, microbial contaminants, and fulvic acid concentration.• Avoid raw, unprocessed Shilajit from unreliable sources.

Formulations• Shilajit comes in resin, powder, and capsule forms. Resin and standardized powders offer more precise dosing.• Capsules may contain fillers—always check the ingredient list.

Storage• Store in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight.• Keep the lid tightly sealed to preserve potency.

Typical Dosage• Beginner: 250 mg/day (ideally split into morning and afternoon).• Maintenance: 500 mg/day.• Cycle: Some doctors recommend 8–12 weeks on, followed by a 2–4 week break to prevent down-regulation.

6. Lifestyle Strategies to Complement Shilajit

Shilajit is not a magic bullet. Combine it with healthy habits to fully optimize your body:

Balanced Nutrition• Prioritize protein, healthy fats, fiber, and colorful fruits and vegetables.• Minimize ultra-processed foods, refined sugars, and excessive caffeine.

Regular Exercise• Strength training supports healthy testosterone levels.• Moderate cardio (150 minutes/week) helps regulate cortisol and insulin.

Stress Management• Practice mindfulness, deep breathing, or yoga to keep cortisol in check.• Aim for 7–9 hours of quality sleep per night.

Hydration and Mineral Balance• Drink plenty of water and consider electrolyte support if you sweat heavily.• Magnesium and zinc—key cofactors in hormone production—should be at optimal levels.

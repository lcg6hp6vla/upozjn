Understanding Skin Laxity: Biological Mechanisms

Skin laxity is a natural consequence of aging, driven primarily by the gradual decline of two essential proteins: collagen and elastin. Collagen provides structure and firmness, while elastin contributes to skin’s resilience and ability to "bounce back."

With age, collagen degradation leads to weakened skin architecture and the accumulation of fragmented collagen, one of the hallmark processes of aging skin. Simultaneously, elastin loss reduces skin elasticity, contributing to sagging, drooping, and a fatigued appearance.

Other factors accelerating skin laxity include:

Rapid weight loss (e.g., through treatments like Ozempic®)

Hormonal changes, especially during menopause, which reduce facial volume

Environmental stressors and lifestyle factors (e.g., UV exposure, smoking)

The combined effects result in visible skin laxity, diminished facial contours, and a more aged overall appearance.

# Ryan Lochte Finally Sets the Record Straight on the ‘Lochtegate’ Scandal

Swimmer Ryan Lochte made global headlines during the 2016 Olympics when he and his teammates stated that they had been robbed at gunpoint in Rio de Janeiro. Lochte claimed that a gun had been pointed at his head. However, conflicting statements from witnesses ultimately led to Lochte admitting that this had not been the case. Following the scandal, dubbed "Lochtegate" by the press, Lochte was suspended from competing for 10 months, and lost all of his major endorsement deals.

In an interview with Graham Bensinger, Lochte clarified that aside from the one lie about the gun (it was not pointed at his head, but at his chest from around 4 feet away), he did not fabricate any of the events, and accepted responsibility for his part in the scandal. He also shared how the prolonged backlash to "Lochtegate" affected him.

"The thing I hate the most is it happened during the Olympics and it took away these other athletes' glory," he said. "This took over everything. I got home and I couldn't turn on the TV without seeing my face on there. I shouldn't have gone on social media, and I did, and I was getting death threats, being like 'you need to die, go kill yourself, you're scum.' Just the worst of the worst. The thing that got me the most was little kids being like 'I used to look up to you and now I don't.' That's what got me."

Then, in 2018, Lochte faced another suspension, this time for 14 months, after getting an IV of B12, due to the volume of the infusion, which exceeded the USADA's permitted levels. This prompted another dark period for the athlete. "It was just one bad thing after another that was happening, and I was just on a spiral staircase going down, down, down," he said. "I was always getting in trouble, I was drinking too much, my head was not in the right spot... I was like, if I keep going, I'm going to wind up dead."

He added that there have been positive aspects to the last four years, saying that had it not been for the intense circumstances surrounding his situation at the 2016 Olympics, he and then-girlfriend Kayla Rae Reid may not have ended up growing closer and getting married. Lochte also credits the second suspension with being the wake-up call he needed, and sought help at rehab. "Everything happens for a reason," he said.

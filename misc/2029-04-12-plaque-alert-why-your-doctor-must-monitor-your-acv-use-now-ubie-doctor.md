Understanding Plaque: Why Your Doctor Monitors ACV Use

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Arterial plaque—a buildup of cholesterol, calcium, and other substances—narrows arteries and raises the risk of angina, heart attack, and stroke. Many people ask whether apple cider vinegar (ACV) can dissolve plaque or lower cholesterol naturally.

While ACV is widely promoted for heart health, human evidence remains limited, and overuse may cause side effects or interact with medications like diuretics, insulin, or digoxin. Doctors typically monitor ACV use by reviewing supplements and blood work to prevent interactions and ensure it supports—not replaces—your heart health plan.

What the Research Shows

Animal StudiesIn rats fed high-cholesterol diets, ACV lowered LDL and total cholesterol. While promising, animal data don't always translate to humans.

Small Human TrialsA few short-term studies show mild reductions in cholesterol and triglycerides with daily ACV. However:

Sample sizes were small.

Participants often followed strict diets.

Results varied widely.

Mechanisms Proposed

Acetic Acid: May prevent cholesterol synthesis in the liver.

Antioxidants: Polyphenols in ACV could protect blood vessels from damage.

pH Changes: Some suggest acidity helps "dissolve" fatty deposits, but there's no direct proof in humans.

Potential Risks of ACV Use

While generally safe for most people, long-term or excessive ACV use can cause:

Tooth enamel erosion (due to acidity)

Throat irritation

Digestive upset (nausea, diarrhea)

Lowered potassium levels

Interactions with medications (e.g., diuretics, insulin, digoxin)

Because these risks can affect heart health indirectly, doctors may want to monitor:

Blood potassium

Kidney function

Medication levels

How Doctors Monitor ACV Use

To ensure safety, your doctor may:

Review Your SupplementsAlways list ACV under "herbs and supplements" when asked.

Check Blood WorkMonitoring electrolytes and kidney function helps catch issues early.

Assess Medication InteractionsACV can amplify or blunt drug effects. Your doctor will adjust doses if needed.

Ask About Dosage and FormLiquid ACV is more acidic than capsules—important when considering side effects.

Evaluate Dental HealthIf you report tooth sensitivity, your doctor may refer you to a dentist.

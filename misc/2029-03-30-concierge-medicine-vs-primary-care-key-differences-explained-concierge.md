Why NAD+ Matters for Brain Function

As we age, NAD+ levels naturally decline—contributing to symptoms like brain fog, fatigue, and even cognitive decline. Because NAD+ plays such a significant role in cellular energy production, organs like the brain can decline in function when NAD levels decrease.

Research shows that lower levels of NAD in the brain have been linked to neurodegenerative diseases like Alzheimer’s, dementia and Parkinson’s. [1] An understanding of the role of NAD+ in Neuronal Mitochondria further explains why this matters for brain function.

The Role of NAD+ in Neuronal Mitochondria

Neurons are highly energy-dependent cells, and their proper function relies on healthy mitochondria—the energy-producing organelles in cells. NAD+ is a key coenzyme in mitochondrial respiration, the process by which cells convert nutrients into ATP (the energy currency of the cell). In neurons:

NAD+ supports oxidative phosphorylation, the primary method by which mitochondria produce energy in the brain.

It helps maintain mitochondrial membrane potential, which is vital for ATP synthesis and neuron viability.

NAD+ activates sirtuins (especially SIRT1 and SIRT3), enzymes that regulate mitochondrial biogenesis, DNA repair, and the cell’s resistance to oxidative stress.

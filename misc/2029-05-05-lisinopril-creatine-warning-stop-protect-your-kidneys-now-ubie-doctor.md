# Lisinopril & Creatine Warning: Stop! Protect Your Kidneys Now | Ubie Doctor's Note

Published on: 5/6/2026

BP Meds & Creatine: Managing Kidney Health on Lisinopril

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Can you take lisinopril with creatine? Yes, in most cases, adults with normal kidney function can safely combine lisinopril and creatine when they stay well-hydrated, monitor blood pressure regularly, check kidney lab values (BUN, creatinine, eGFR), and avoid other nephrotoxic medications like NSAIDs.

Key factors to consider include your baseline kidney health, hydration status, exercise intensity, and any other medications you take. Because both lisinopril and creatine can independently affect kidney markers, individual risk varies.

Creatine and Lisinopril Safety: Managing Kidney Health

High blood pressure (hypertension) and athletic supplements often overlap in active adults. Lisinopril, a commonly prescribed blood pressure medication, and creatine, a popular performance enhancer, both involve kidney processing. Understanding Creatine and Lisinopril safety is key to protecting your renal health while achieving fitness and cardiovascular goals.

Lisinopril is cleared through the kidneys. Changes in kidney function can affect how the body handles this medication—and vice versa.

What Is Creatine?

Creatine is a naturally occurring compound stored in muscles and the brain. Supplemental creatine monohydrate is one of the most researched and widely used sports nutrition products. Benefits include:

Improved strength and power output

Enhanced muscle recovery

Support for high-intensity exercise

After ingestion, creatine converts to phosphocreatine in muscle cells, then to creatinine—a waste product excreted by the kidneys.

Why Kidney Function Matters

The kidneys filter blood to remove waste, regulate fluid balance, and manage electrolytes. Both lisinopril and creatine can influence kidney workload:

Lisinopril alters kidney blood flow to reduce blood pressure.

Creatine supplementation raises blood creatinine levels, a key marker of kidney function tests.

Monitoring ensures you maintain healthy glomerular filtration rate (GFR) and avoid unnecessary stress on renal tissue.

Creatine and Lisinopril Safety: What the Research Says

Current evidence suggests that, in healthy individuals, creatine supplementation up to 5 grams per day for several months does not compromise kidney function. However:

Elevated serum creatinine from creatine may mimic impaired kidney function on lab tests.

Lisinopril's blood-pressure–lowering effect depends on stable kidney filtration.

Key points from studies:

No long-term renal damage seen in athletes using creatine under medical supervision.

Small, transient increases in serum creatinine are common and expected.

Combining ACE inhibitors with other nephrotoxic drugs (e.g., NSAIDs) poses greater risk.

Overall, Creatine and Lisinopril safety appears acceptable in individuals with normal baseline kidney function—but caution and monitoring are essential.

Who Is at Higher Risk?

Not everyone on lisinopril can—or should—take creatine. Increased concern arises if you have:

Pre-existing kidney disease or reduced GFR

Diabetes with kidney involvement

Dehydration or chronic illness

Concurrent use of other kidney-affecting medications (e.g., diuretics, NSAIDs)

If you fall into any of these categories, creatine use may require extra caution or avoidance.

Practical Tips for Safe Use

If you and your doctor decide to combine creatine with lisinopril, follow these guidelines:

Baseline Kidney Assessment

Obtain serum creatinine and GFR before starting creatine.

Repeat labs 4–8 weeks after beginning supplementation.

Start Low and Go Slow

Begin with 3–5 g/day of creatine monohydrate.

Avoid "loading" phases (20 g/day) if you have any kidney concerns.

Stay Hydrated

Aim for 2–3 liters of water per day, depending on activity level.

Monitor urine color—pale straw color indicates adequate hydration.

Avoid Nephrotoxic Agents

Limit nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs).

Discuss other supplements or medications with your doctor.

Monitor Blood Pressure and Labs

Check blood pressure regularly while on lisinopril.

Schedule follow-up renal panels and electrolyte tests.

Listen to Your Body

Report symptoms like swelling, sudden weight gain, fatigue, or changes in urine output.

These could signal altered kidney or heart function.

Recognizing Early Signs of Kidney Stress

Even with careful monitoring, subtle signs may indicate kidney strain:

Unusual fatigue or weakness

Back or flank pain

Changes in urine frequency, color, or foaming

Swelling in ankles, feet, or around the eyes

When to Avoid Creatine on Lisinopril

Discuss creatine use with your doctor if you have:

Stage 3–5 chronic kidney disease (eGFR below 60 mL/min/1.73 m²)

Uncontrolled hypertension or heart conditions beyond lisinopril management

Recent acute kidney injury or ongoing renal replacement therapy

Your physician may recommend alternative strategies for muscle performance or blood pressure control.

Balancing Benefits and Risks

For many adults, the combination of lisinopril and moderate-dose creatine can be safe when:

Kidney function is normal at baseline

You adhere to hydration and lab-monitoring protocols

Your medical team is aware of all supplements and medications

However, no supplement or medication is completely risk-free. Honest communication with your healthcare provider ensures you reap benefits while minimizing potential harm.

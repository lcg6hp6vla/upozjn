# Dry, Gritty Eyes That Won't Let Up: What Doctors Look For | Ubie Doctor's Note

Dry, Gritty Eyes That Won't Let Up: What Doctors Look For

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Persistent dry, gritty eyes typically occur when tears fail to lubricate the eye surface properly. Common causes include aging, hormonal changes, environmental triggers, autoimmune diseases, certain medications, and nutritional deficiencies. Doctors evaluate accompanying symptoms such as redness, burning, blurred vision, and tear quality to identify the root cause.

Diagnosis usually involves a detailed medical history, specialized tests (Schirmer test, tear break-up time, ocular surface staining), meibomian gland evaluation, and lab work to rule out underlying conditions. Treatment options range from artificial tears and prescription eye drops to lifestyle adjustments and targeted therapies.

Because dry, gritty eyes can stem from many overlapping causes, identifying the right next step on your own can be difficult. Explanation

Persistent dry, gritty eyes can interfere with daily life—making reading, driving and even blinking uncomfortable. If you've tried over-the-counter drops and lifestyle tweaks without relief, it's time to understand what's happening and what doctors look for.

What Causes Chronic Dry Eyes

Chronic dry eyes (also called dry eye disease) occur when your tears don't properly lubricate and nourish the eye surface. Common causes include:

Age and Hormones• Tear production naturally decreases after age 50• Menopause and androgen deficiency can reduce tear film quality

Environmental Factors• Air conditioning, heating or dry climates• Extended screen use (computer, phone) without breaks• Wind, pollution or airborne irritants

Medical Conditions• Autoimmune diseases (e.g., Sjögren's syndrome, rheumatoid arthritis)• Blepharitis (inflamed eyelid margins)• Meibomian gland dysfunction (poor oil layer in tears)

Symptoms That Often Accompany Gritty Eyes

Doctors ask about other symptoms to paint a full picture of your eye health:

Redness or irritation

Burning or stinging sensation

Blurred vision or fluctuating vision

Light sensitivity (photophobia)

Excessive tearing (reflex tearing from dryness)

Mucus strings or eye discharge

Eyelid heaviness or swelling

Tracking when and where symptoms occur can help identify triggers (e.g., reading at night, working in an office, after long drives).

What Doctors Do: The Diagnostic Approach

When you see an eye doctor (optometrist or ophthalmologist), they follow a step-by-step process:

Medical and Ocular History

Review current and past medical conditions

List all medications and supplements

Ask about lifestyle: screen time, environment, diet

Note previous eye surgeries or injuries

Symptom Questionnaire

Standardized surveys gauge severity and impact on daily life

Helps differentiate mild discomfort from moderate or severe disease

External Eye Exam

Check eyelids, lashes and surface for inflammation or debris

Evaluate tear film with a slit lamp microscope

Tear Production and Quality Tests

Schirmer Test: measures tear volume with a small strip of paper under the lower eyelid

Tear Break-Up Time (TBUT): fluorescein dye reveals how quickly tears evaporate

Osmolarity Testing: checks tear salt concentration (high osmolarity suggests dryness)

Ocular Surface Staining

Dyes (fluorescein, lissamine green) highlight damaged or exposed cells on cornea and conjunctiva

Meibomian Gland Evaluation

Apply gentle pressure to eyelid margin to assess oil flow

Infrared imaging may show blocked glands

Allergy or Inflammation Assessment

Exam for allergic conjunctivitis, atopic eye disease

Evaluate for blepharitis (eyelid inflammation)

Laboratory Tests (if systemic disease suspected)

Blood tests for autoimmune markers (ANA, rheumatoid factor, anti-Ro/La antibodies)

Vitamin levels (e.g., vitamin A, D)

Differential Diagnoses

Doctors consider other causes of gritty eyes, such as:

Conjunctivitis (bacterial or viral pink eye)

Corneal abrasions or ulcers

Eyelash mites (Demodex)

Contact lens–related dryness

Ocular surface tumors (rare)

When to Seek Further Help

Most dry eye symptoms improve with conservative measures. However, contact a doctor promptly if you experience:

Severe eye pain or headache

Sudden vision changes (blurring, halos, loss of vision)

Light sensitivity that worsens

Persistent redness or discharge

Signs of infection (fever, swollen eyelids)

Final Thoughts

Dry, gritty eyes can be frustrating, but a systematic evaluation usually uncovers the cause and leads to relief. Keep track of your symptoms, share your medical history and follow your doctor's recommendations.

Speak to a doctor about any eye symptoms that could be serious or life threatening. Early intervention protects your vision and overall eye health.

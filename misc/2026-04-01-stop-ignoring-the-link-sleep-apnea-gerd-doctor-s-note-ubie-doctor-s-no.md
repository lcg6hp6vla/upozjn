# Stop Ignoring the Link: Sleep Apnea & GERD | Doctor’s Note | Ubie Doctor's Note

How is sleep apnea linked to acid reflux or GERD?

[Sleep Apnea GERD](https://glp-insights.xianfengchem.com/blog/8309647106.html)

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Sleep apnea and acid reflux (GERD) are closely connected conditions that often worsen each other, particularly at night. Sleep apnea disrupts normal breathing during sleep, creating pressure changes in the chest and abdomen that can push stomach acid up into the esophagus and throat. In turn, acid reflux can irritate the airway, making apnea episodes more frequent and severe. This two-way relationship means treating one condition often improves the other.

[Apnea GERD Doctor](https://brainmessenger.xianfengchem.com/)

Sleep apnea and gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) are often linked because they can affect each other, especially during the night. When someone has sleep apnea, they stop breathing for short periods during sleep. This can cause pressure changes in the chest and throat, which might allow stomach acid to move up into the esophagus more easily, leading to GERD symptoms.

[Link Sleep](https://cortexcompass.jcesqc8.com/)

According to the research, GERD can also make sleep apnea worse. This happens because the acid can irritate the throat and airways, making it harder to breathe properly at night. People with GERD might wake up more often because of discomfort from acid reflux, which can disturb their sleep and make apnea episodes more frequent.

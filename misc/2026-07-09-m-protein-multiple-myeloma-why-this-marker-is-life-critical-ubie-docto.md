What role does M protein play in the diagnosis of multiple myeloma?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

M protein, or monoclonal protein, is an abnormal antibody produced by cancerous plasma cells in the bone marrow. Detecting M protein in the blood or urine is a key diagnostic marker for multiple myeloma, a type of blood cancer, as well as related conditions like MGUS and Waldenström macroglobulinemia. Doctors typically identify M protein through serum protein electrophoresis (SPEP) or immunofixation tests, and rising levels can indicate disease progression.

Explanation

M protein, also known as monoclonal protein, plays an important role in diagnosing multiple myeloma. Multiple myeloma is a cancer of plasma cells, which are a type of white blood cell that normally helps fight infections. In this disease, cancerous plasma cells produce an abnormal protein called M protein.

How M Protein Helps in Diagnosis:

[Why This](https://thymicshield.dns0663.com/blog/0485993478.html)

Detection: Doctors look for M protein in the blood or urine to help diagnose multiple myeloma. High levels of this protein can indicate the presence of the disease.

Monitoring: Once diagnosed, the amount of M protein is also used to monitor the disease's progression and response to treatment.

By measuring M protein levels, doctors can not only confirm a diagnosis but also keep track of how well a patient is responding to treatment.

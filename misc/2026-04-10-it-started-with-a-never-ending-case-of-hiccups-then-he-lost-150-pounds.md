# It Started With a Never-Ending Case of Hiccups. Then He Lost 150 Pounds.

I’VE ALWAYS LOVED lifting weights. But doing cardio? Not so much. Running, biking, even walking—it never appealed to me. So while I’ve always had some muscle on me, it was never really defined (and, honestly, buried underneath a bit of a belly). That caught up with me, though, during the COVID lockdown in 2020 when I was quarantined with my wife in upstate New York. Not only was I stuck at home, where I had a few more beers than I probably should have, but I also became a dad at the age of 40 for the second time—something that completely threw off my eating habits.

By early 2024, I was 318 pounds.

This was also happening while we were relocating to Florida where my wife got a teaching job offer at a college. I didn’t have worked lined up, but I figured something would turn up eventually. That something ended up being a job selling pools—and it’s not hyperbole to say that it ended up saving my life.

I’VE HAD FRIENDS who weighed 300 pounds die in their 40s, so I knew the dangerous risks of being heavy. And it was already starting to show. I developed sleep apnea. I had health scares. Once, I even landed in the emergency room when my blood pressure spiked to something like 220/160.

The doctor had looked at me and said, “The last person who came in here with blood pressure like that, we put them in a helicopter and they died.” It was a subtle warning to make a change. But honestly, I didn’t until I developed a hiccuping problem.

‘Hiccups?’ you might ask. That was your motivation?

But this wasn’t your usual hiccups. I’m talking hiccups every hour. Hiccups multiple times an hour. Uncontrollable hiccups that lasted five, six, sometimes ten times in a row. I couldn’t get through a conversation without having to hiccup. I had to schedule calls and presentations for early morning and fast until it was over, because fasting was the only thing that was even a little bit helpful. My wife told me I even hiccuped in my sleep.

There were no answers for it. I saw doctors, specialists; I had scopes and brain scans done but no one knew what was causing them. It got to where I was seriously considering a procedure to clip the phrenic nerve in my diaphragm—but that still wasn’t guaranteed to work.

Looking back, I think my body was sending me a message that I needed to change my life. And with some luck I had a new means to shave off the excess pounds. But as it happened, I also got lucky.

I ended up getting hired as in sales at Latham Pool, a Florida-based fiberglass pool manufacturing company. To show and model the product to dealers, Latham offered me a great deal to put a pool in my backyard. Not only did a pool boost my home value, it was a great way to work out from the comfort of my own backyard.

I STARTED SWIMMING on March 18, 2024. It was two weeks after the pool was installed. My goal was to do laps for 30 minutes a day.

In the beginning, I couldn’t do 30 minutes all at once. Instead, I did 10 minutes, waited a few hours, did another 10, waited, and finished the last 10. And when I say ‘laps,’ I mean that loosely. In the early days, moving around was enough for me to call it a lap. But I aimed to be consistent—30 minutes a day, every day.

Even though I never liked cardio, I quickly warmed up to swimming. There was something about swimming that felt different—and I’m not just saying that because I work for a pool company, I swear!

Walking was never fun because of the way my lower back would ache and feel swollen. Now this doesn’t mean swimming is easy. But it was easier on my body, and soon I started doing actual laps around the pool. Soon I could do 30 minutes without stopping. And now?

Now, I’m doing 60 or 70 laps at a time, for an hour and a half—past my original goal of 30 minutes. If you see me in my pool, I’m likely doing freestyle and backstrokes, but I’ll also mix it up and tread water or do some dolphin kicks.

[Never-Ending Case](https://repair-cascade.jcesqc8.com/)

Once my stamina grew, I started using my home gym more, too. I even threw in some core exercises.

Beyond the pool, I also made another important change. I gave up alcohol. Now, I never had a serious problem with it, but I know I don’t make good food choices when I drink. Once I have one or two vodkas under my belt, I lose all desire to eat chicken breast and broccoli and instead reach for the pizza and chicken wings.

[With Never-Ending](https://regeneration-desk.hbsnzj.com/)

I also cut back on dessert and now focus mostly on getting enough protein and fiber. I’m talking about the basics, like chicken, vegetables, and fruit. That doesn’t mean my meals are boring. Instead, I follow an 80/20 rule—nothing is totally off-limits, but I eat a primarily healthy diet. The moments when I do crave something sweet, I mix a high-quality protein powder with a packet of zero-sugar pudding mix—and the craving is done in 10 minutes.

IT'S BEEN A little over a year since I started swimming and I’m proud to say I’ve lost 147 pounds. And it shows.

I’ve dropped from a size 40 to a size 30. While I’ve missed a day or two here and there (hey, one of those days I had the flu!) I’ve stayed consistent.

It’s the one piece of advice I’d offer to anyone who’s trying to lose weight: Be consistent. I’ve tried—and failed—to diet in the past. When I look back on it, the mistake was the lack of consistency. I’d skip a day here, and another one there, and eventually, I’d stop trying to exercise or eat well altogether.

Things are so much different now that I’m thinner. It’s even easier to sit for longer periods of time. It used to be hard for me to drive four or five hours for work; now, it’s no big deal. My kids—now 6 and 3 years old (yes, we had another one)—also love to swim. It’s nice to hop in the pool with them after work. They’re strong swimmers, too.

My cholesterol and blood pressure are also in a healthy range now. My sleep apnea is gone. And best of all, those horrible hiccups are finally gone. They disappeared five weeks after I started swimming, when the weight was starting to come off.

I’m still not sure why I got hiccups in the first place. Neither do the doctors. All I know is I’m going to keep swimming and eating healthily so they never come back.

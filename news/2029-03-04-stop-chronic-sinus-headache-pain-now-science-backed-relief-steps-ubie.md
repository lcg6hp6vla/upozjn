Explanation

Chronic sinus headaches are a common complaint, often described as a constant, dull ache or pressure around the forehead, cheeks, eyes and sometimes the upper teeth. Unlike occasional tension headaches or migraines, these headaches persist for weeks or months, affecting quality of life. Understanding what drives these head pains—and what you can do about them—is the first step toward lasting relief.

What Are Chronic Sinus Headaches?

Chronic sinus headaches arise when the sinus cavities (air-filled spaces in your skull) become inflamed or blocked for an extended period—usually 12 weeks or longer. The inflammation leads to:

Swelling of the sinus lining

Thickened mucus that can't drain properly

Increased pressure inside the sinuses

This pressure pushes on surrounding nerves and bone, which your brain interprets as pain or "headache."

Key Points

"Chronic" means symptoms persist for 12 weeks or more.

Sinus cavities include the frontal, maxillary, ethmoid and sphenoid sinuses.

The Science of Pressure

When sinuses are healthy, they fill with air and drain mucus seamlessly into your nose. If drainage is blocked:

Mucus Builds Up: Thick, sticky mucus collects, raising internal pressure.

Inflammation Spreads: The lining of the sinus cavities becomes more irritated and swollen.

Nerve Activation: Pressure stimulates pain receptors in the sinus walls, sending pain signals to the brain.

Over time, this cycle of mucus buildup and inflammation can sensitize nerves, making even minor changes in pressure or temperature feel painful.

Common Causes

Several factors can trigger or perpetuate chronic sinus inflammation and headaches:

Chronic Sinusitis (long-term sinus infection)

Nasal allergies (hay fever, indoor allergens)

Nasal polyps or other structural blockages

Deviated septum (crooked nasal partition)

Environmental irritants (smoke, pollution, strong odors)

Immune system issues that make infections more likely

Often, more than one factor is at play—for example, allergies setting the stage for repeated sinus infections.

Signs and Symptoms

Sinus headaches share features with other headache types. Look for these red flags pointing to a sinus origin:

Facial pressure or fullness, especially in the cheeks, forehead or behind the eyes

Pain worsens when bending forward or lying down

Nasal congestion or runny nose

Thick, discolored mucus (yellow or green)

Reduced sense of smell or taste

Post-nasal drip (mucus dripping down the throat)

Possible fever or general fatigue if an infection is present

Diagnosis: Separating Sinus Headaches from Other Types

Because migraines and tension headaches can also cause facial pain or pressure, proper diagnosis is crucial:

Medical History & Physical Exam

Your doctor will ask about symptom duration, triggers and past treatments.

They'll examine your nose with a lighted instrument to look for swelling, polyps or discharge.

Imaging (when simple exam isn't conclusive)

CT scan of the sinuses shows detailed images of blockage or structural issues.

MRI is less common but can rule out other causes if symptoms are unusual.

Allergy Testing

Skin or blood tests to identify environmental triggers.

Endoscopy

A thin, flexible scope inserted into the nose provides a direct look at the sinus openings.

Early and accurate diagnosis ensures you avoid unnecessary therapies (e.g., migraine medications) and focus on treatments that reduce sinus inflammation and pressure.

Lifestyle and Prevention

Preventing flare-ups of chronic sinus headaches often involves long-term strategies:

Keep indoor humidity around 40–50% to prevent overly dry or moist air.

Use high-efficiency particulate air (HEPA) filters to reduce allergens.

When to Seek Immediate Help

While most chronic sinus headaches aren't emergencies, seek prompt medical attention if you experience:

Sudden, severe headache unlike anything before

High fever (above 102°F) with stiff neck or confusion

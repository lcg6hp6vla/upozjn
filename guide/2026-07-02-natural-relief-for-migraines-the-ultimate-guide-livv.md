What Are Migraines?

While migraines and headaches both cause head pain, they are quite different. Migraines are a type of headache, but they have unique symptoms and intensity. Where tension headaches typically involve dull, steady pain, migraines often involve intense, throbbing pain, nausea, and sensitivity to light and sound. Migraines may last anywhere from a few hours to several days and can be debilitating, requiring you to lie down in a dark, quiet room.

Different Types of Migraines

There are several types of migraines, each with its own symptoms. Understanding which type of migraine you experience can help guide treatment.

Migraines with aura: Visual disturbances such as flashing lights or zigzag lines precede these migraines. The aura typically lasts about 20 to 30 minutes before the headache begins.

Migraines without aura: The most common type of migraine, with the headache starting suddenly and progressing, often without any warning signs.

Chronic migraines: Chronic migraines occur 15 or more days per month and last for at least three months. They can significantly impact daily life.

Silent migraines: Also known as acephalgic migraines, these occur without the headache pain but still involve other symptoms like aura or nausea.

What Causes Migraines?

Migraines are thought to be caused by a combination of genetic and environmental factors. Although the exact cause isn’t fully understood, several factors contribute to the onset of a migraine, including changes in the brain, neurotransmitters, and blood flow.

Underlying Triggers of Migraines

Several triggers can provoke a migraine attack. These triggers vary from person to person, but common causes include:

Hormonal changes, especially in women, due to menstruation, pregnancy, and menopause

Stress and anxiety

Dietary factors, including certain foods, alcohol, or caffeine

Environmental factors, including strong smells, loud noises, or bright lights

Sleep disturbances or irregular sleep patterns

Genetics: Migraines tend to run in families, suggesting a genetic predisposition
